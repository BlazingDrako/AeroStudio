# Flight & Atmosphere

[← Back to the README](../../README.md)

The atmosphere tabs share one solver, `js/solvers/atmosphere.js` (no DOM), which the Jet Engine Cycles, Shocks & Nozzles and
Vehicle aero tools now also use. A US / SI switch (ft, kt or m, m/s) is remembered in the browser; calculations run in SI.

- **Atmosphere**: US Standard Atmosphere 1976 (identical to ICAO ISA below 32 km) from 0 to 86 km: seven layers in geopotential
  height, hydrostatic pressure, Sutherland viscosity, conductivity, mean free path, number density, scale height and gravity.
  A temperature offset (ISA ± ΔT) keeps the standard pressure, so the tool shows pressure, density and true altitude (the
  hypsometric thickness of the warmer or colder column). A draggable column of the atmosphere shows the layers, the ozone
  layer, the temperature profile and landmarks (Everest, airliner cruise, Concorde, Armstrong limit, U-2, SR-71, balloons);
  any property can be plotted against altitude; the table exports to CSV.
- **Airspeeds**: CAS, EAS, TAS and Mach from any one of them through the pitot impact pressure, isentropic below Mach 1 and
  the Rayleigh pitot formula above it; dynamic and impact pressure, total temperature and unit Reynolds number; a speed ladder
  showing what separates each pair; the conversion step by step; and a CAS/Mach climb schedule with its crossover altitude.
- **Wind & turbulence**: Dryden gusts (MIL-F-8785C / MIL-HDBK-1797) with low-altitude scales and intensities from the wind at
  20 ft; above 2,000 ft the scale is 1,750 ft and the intensity is held at its 1,000 ft value (a simplification of the
  probability-of-exceedance tables). Gusts are generated by stepping first- and second-order shaping filters exactly
  from seeded white noise. An animated aircraft flies through the frozen gust field; gust histories, the quasi-steady gust
  load factor Δn = ρVC_Lα·w/(2W/S), Welch spectra against the Dryden spectra, σ and L against height, and the log-law wind
  profile for six surface roughnesses.

The three performance tabs share `js/solvers/performance.js` (no DOM) and one aircraft selection (aircraft, gross mass, fuel,
ΔT, thrust rating). Aircraft are teaching models with representative public figures, named "-class": a Cessna 172-class
light single, a 15 m sailplane, an A320-class airliner, an illustrative CCA-class combat drone and an F-16-class fighter.
Drag is a parabolic polar with Lock's fourth-power drag rise (subsonic) or a transonic peak and supersonic K growth
(fighter). Engines use Mattingly's installed thrust lapse and TSFC (high-bypass, dry and afterburning low-bypass
turbofans, the fighter's normal-shock inlet recovery), and the Gagg–Ferrar power lapse with a fixed-pitch propeller.

- **Performance**: forces in steady flight drawn on the aircraft (T and D magnified) with a clickable speed tape; drag
  and power curves (parasite, induced, available) with Vmd, Vmp, Vx, Vy and Vmax; the climb hodograph; speeds and best
  climb against altitude with service and absolute ceilings and time to climb; range and endurance by integrating the
  cruise fuel burn (max-range, long-range 99% or endurance speeds); specific range and payload–range; takeoff and landing
  distances (Raymer's ground roll, transition and climb to the 35/50 ft screen, approach, flare, free roll and braking),
  with field elevation, hot days and wind. Sailplanes get best glide, minimum sink and glide range instead.
- **Turn & V–n**: the turn-rate "doghouse" (energy–maneuverability) chart with stall and structural limits, the corner
  speed, Ps contours, constant-radius and constant-load-factor lines; a to-scale picture of the sustained and
  instantaneous turn circles and the banked lift vector; the V–n diagram with maneuver envelope and FAR 23/25 gust lines
  (Pratt alleviation factor).
- **Envelope**: the altitude–Mach map of specific excess power (colour and contours, marching squares), the Ps = 0
  level-flight envelope, stall, VNE/VD and Mach limits, energy-height lines, Rutowski's minimum-time energy climb to a
  point you click, Ps slices against Mach and the climb path against time.

The two stability tabs share `js/solvers/stability.js` (no DOM). Stability derivatives are estimated from each aircraft's
geometry (`GEOMETRY`: wing, tails, fuselage, ailerons, CG limits, inertias) with the classical methods: DATCOM/Helmbold
lift slopes, downwash, Perkins & Hage fuselage moment, tail-volume damping plus DATCOM wing pitch damping, sidewash, strip
roll damping and aileron power (Nelson; Etkin & Reid). The tail incidence is rigged so each aircraft trims at mid CG with
the elevator at zero at its starting condition. Derivatives are subsonic (the condition is capped at Mach 0.9). The
F-16-class fighter is calibrated to about 4% unstable at 35% MAC (strake and blended-body lift are not in the estimate).
CG, tail sizes and dihedral can be changed ("what if").

- **Stability & trim**: the aircraft as a balance (wing-body lift, tail load, weight) with the CG, neutral point and
  maneuver point on a magnified MAC with the certified CG range; Cm–α for forward, current, aft and neutral CG; trim
  elevator against speed; elevator per g; all longitudinal and lateral-directional derivatives.
- **Dynamic modes**: longitudinal [u, w, q, θ] and lateral [β, p, r, φ] state matrices, eigenvalues (Faddeev–LeVerrier
  characteristic polynomial, Durand–Kerner roots) and eigenvectors; root loci against CG, speed or altitude; an
  animation of any mode from its eigenvector, or of an elevator pulse / rudder doublet (RK4), with time histories;
  MIL-F-8785C Category B levels for every mode and the CAP–ζ chart.
